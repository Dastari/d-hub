Here is the proposed direction for our fictional communications drawer.

THE FEED

Keep everything together in one chronological feed. Work, personal mail and
supported conversations should share a visual language. Source names help us
understand context, but should not compete for attention with different brand
colours. Focus profiles filter the same feed.

THE COMPOSER

The place where a reply will go should always be obvious. Start a draft here,
then select another message. Notice how the reply header stays with this
conversation while the preview changes. An attachment alone also locks it.

Clearing the text does not unlock the target. Use an explicit discard or
save-and-switch action when you want to change the conversation you are editing.

READING

Longer conversations deserve room. The reader can show the complete message
without borrowing the drawer's reply context. Search, scroll and close it:
the draft should still be exactly where you left it.

VISUAL RHYTHM

Use space rather than boxes. A restrained shared palette, soft selection
surfaces and small braille activity indicators can make the interface feel
alive without making it busy. Reduced motion should always be available.

ATTACHMENTS

The prototype uses a harmless text fixture. File-manager copy/paste and image
graphics come in later milestones, after the draft lifecycle is proven.

NEXT REVIEW

1. Type a multiline reply, including café, 東京 and 👩🏽‍💻.
2. Browse another message and change the focus profile.
3. Add a fixture attachment and simulate an incoming message.
4. Exercise mock success, failure and uncertain acceptance.
5. Quit and reopen the same demo data directory to restore the draft.

No mail or messaging provider is connected. All sends in this prototype are
local simulations, and all people and addresses are fictional.
